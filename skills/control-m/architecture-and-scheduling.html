Control-M roadmap / 01

Architecture and scheduling

Understand the main Control-M components and how calendars, scheduling rules, and time zones determine when work becomes eligible.

ComponentsSchedule examplePractice

01 / Components

Connect definitions to execution.

Enterprise Manager

Provides centralized visibility and management across the Control-M environment, subject to the product deployment and version.

Control-M/Server

Manages scheduling and job workflow state for its environment and communicates with agents.

Agent

Runs work on a host and reports job status back to the scheduling environment.

Calendars and time zones

Business days, holidays, time zones, and daylight-saving changes affect the actual eligible run time.

02 / Scheduling example

Schedule from business conditions.

Business calendar

Define the valid operating days and exception dates needed for the workflow.

Run window

Set an intended start window and business time zone; confirm host and server time assumptions.

Predecessor condition

Require upstream data readiness instead of relying only on a fixed delay.

Deadline and alert

Define when the result is due and who should be notified if the flow is at risk.

Example: a weekday report should use its business calendar and wait for the successful completion condition from the overnight load before running.

03 / Practice

Interview checks.

What is the purpose of an agent?

An agent runs configured work on a host and communicates execution state to the Control-M scheduling environment.

Why use a calendar instead of a simple daily schedule?

Calendars represent business days, holidays, and exceptions, making schedules reflect operational rules rather than a fixed clock alone.

What time-zone issues can break a schedule?

Server/host differences, daylight-saving changes, and calendar time-zone assumptions can shift eligible times. Confirm the business time zone and test boundary dates.
