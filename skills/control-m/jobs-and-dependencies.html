Control-M roadmap / 02

Jobs and dependencies

Model jobs as a readable workflow with explicit conditions, resources, variables, and success or failure paths.

Job designWorkflow examplePractice

01 / Job design

Describe executable work and its contract.

Job definition

Specify the executable or application, host or agent, runtime parameters, owner, and operational standards required by the job type.

Conditions

Use predecessor and successor conditions to represent when work is eligible and which downstream jobs may proceed.

Resources

Declare required resources or concurrency limits where supported so competing jobs do not over-consume shared capacity.

Variables and outputs

Pass batch identifiers, dates, or produced values through supported variables and outputs with clear ownership.

02 / Workflow example

Load, validate, publish.

Load job

Runs the ingestion command and reports completion with a batch identifier.

Validation job

Waits for load success, checks counts and required keys, then sets a validated condition.

Publish job

Runs only after validation success and records the output-ready condition.

Failure path

Routes failures to alert and recovery handling rather than releasing downstream consumers.

Design test: verify what happens when each job fails, reruns, or finishes late; check that success conditions cannot be left over from an earlier batch.

03 / Practice

Interview checks.

Why prefer event or condition dependencies over fixed delays?

Conditions express actual upstream completion and reduce race conditions caused by unpredictable run times. Fixed waits can waste time and still fail when an upstream job runs longer than expected.

How would you prevent stale conditions from releasing a new batch?

Use batch-aware condition naming or supported cleanup and ordering rules so each cycle consumes the right predecessor state. Validate this behavior with overlapping and late runs.

What information should a job definition make clear?

What runs, where it runs, required parameters and resources, expected outputs, owner, schedule or dependency, alerting, and recovery expectations.
