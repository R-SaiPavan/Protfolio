Control-M roadmap / 04

SLAs and operations

Relate workflow health to business deadlines, monitor risk, and communicate status with useful operational measures.

SLA designOperations examplePractice

01 / SLA design

Measure outcomes, not only job success.

Business deadline

Define when the data product or business process must be ready, including its business calendar and time zone.

Critical path

Identify the chain of jobs that determines completion time and which upstream delays threaten the deadline.

Risk and alerting

Set useful warning points before breach, route alerts to owners, and include impact and recovery context.

Service reporting

Track on-time completion, duration trends, failure rate, retry frequency, and recurring bottlenecks.

02 / Operations example

When a reporting chain is at risk.

Detect

Monitor the target completion time and recognize a late upstream extract.

Assess

Use the dependency flow and estimated remaining duration to determine if the deadline is at risk.

Respond

Notify the owner with current status and recovery options; do not skip a required quality gate without approval.

Learn

Record the cause and adjust capacity, scheduling, or dependency design if the pattern repeats.

Operational principle: a green job status is not enough if the downstream data is late, incomplete, or unvalidated.

03 / Practice

Interview checks.

How do you define an SLA for a workflow?

Start with a business-ready deadline, calendar, and time zone. Map the critical path, define warning and breach thresholds, and identify owners and recovery actions.

What metrics help improve batch operations?

On-time completion, job duration, queue/resource waits, failure and retry rates, data freshness, and recurring root causes.

What would you do if a workflow is green but its data is incomplete?

Use data-quality and reconciliation signals, hold downstream publication, identify the missing input or failed validation, and communicate the impact before releasing corrected output.
