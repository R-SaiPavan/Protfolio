Control-M roadmap / 03

Monitoring and recovery

Use job status, logs, alerts, and dependencies to find failures and recover without duplicating downstream work.

MonitoringRecovery examplePractice

01 / Monitoring

Follow a run from status to impact.

Job status

Review current and prior status, start/end times, run attempts, and the condition state of predecessors and successors.

Output and logs

Inspect job output and application logs for the original failure, not only the final scheduler status.

Alerts

Route actionable notifications to the owning team and include job, order, batch, error, and recovery context.

Dependencies

Check whether the delay is a failed job, a late predecessor, a resource wait, or a calendar/schedule rule.

02 / Recovery example

Diagnose before rerunning.

1. Identify scope

Find the failed job, affected batch, completed predecessors, and downstream jobs that are held or already running.

2. Check side effects

Determine whether the job partially wrote data or emitted conditions that may affect a retry.

3. Correct cause

Resolve the source, connection, resource, or application problem before restarting.

4. Resume and reconcile

Restart from the documented safe point and verify row counts, outputs, and downstream readiness.

Rule: a rerun is safe only when the task's side effects and downstream state are understood.

03 / Practice

Interview checks.

A job failed after writing part of its output. What do you do?

Confirm the partial-write behavior, identify the affected batch and downstream state, then use the approved cleanup or idempotent replay procedure. Reconcile results before releasing dependents.

How do you distinguish a failed predecessor from a resource wait?

Inspect job status, order flow, logs, resource availability, and timing. Confirm whether the predecessor completed and emitted the expected condition.

What makes an alert operationally useful?

It identifies the impacted workflow and batch, the failure reason, current downstream impact, owning team, and a link or steps for diagnosis.
